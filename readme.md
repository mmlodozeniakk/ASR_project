**COMPARATIVE EVALUATION OF OPENAI WHISPER FOR AUTOMATIC SPEECH RECOGNITION**:

The aim of this project is to evaluate the performance of OpenAI Whisper across four languages: English, French, Italian and Polish. The study focuses on measuring the Word Error Rate (WER) for each language, examining the discrepancies between the model's transcriptions and the reference transcrips. In order to compliment the quantitative evaluation, a sample of transcriptions was manually annotated and analyzed to provide a more detailed understanding of the errors made by the model. 

**DATASET**
This project uses the VoxPopuli dataset, a large-scale multilingual speech corpus containing recordings of speeches delivered at the European Parliament between the years 2009 and 2020, accompanied by reference transcripts. It provides a suitable basis for evaluating Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) on real-world speech data in multiple languages, since the dataset comprises approximately 1,800 hours of recordings across 16 languages spoken in the Member States. 
The analysis was conducted on following datasets: 
* English (en): 1842 samples: https://huggingface.co/datasets/facebook/voxpopuli/resolve/main/en/test-00000-of-00001.parquet
* French (fr): 1742 samples: https://huggingface.co/datasets/facebook/voxpopuli/resolve/main/fr/test-00000-of-00001.parquet
* Italian (it): 1177 samples: https://huggingface.co/datasets/facebook/voxpopuli/resolve/main/it/test-00000-of-00001.parquet
* Polish (pl): 1831 samples: "https://huggingface.co/datasets/facebook/voxpopuli/resolve/main/pl/test-00000-of-00001.parquet
For the more detailed analysis, the first 20 recordings from each language's dataset were selected for manual transcription and evaluation. The sample size was chosen to balance practicality and efficiency while allowing multiligual analysis. Moreover, using the same number of recordings for each language ensured consistency and permitted to avoid bias. 

**METHODOLOGY**
TRANSCRIPTIONS:
All recordings selected for the study were processed using OpenAI  Whisper: an ASR system trained on 680,000 hours of multilingual audio data collected from a variety of communication contexts. The same model was used for all four languages to ensure consistency in the evaluation process. To ensure both quantitative and qualitative analysis, the predictions generated by Whisper were compared against two types of transcription: the reference ones provided in the VoxPopuli dataset and ones produced manually for the first 20 recordings in each language. The manual transcriptions were created by the author of this study based on direct listening to the audio recordings. 

QUANTATIVE ANALYSIS: 
Transcription quality was quantitatively evaluated using Word Error Rate (WER) that is calcluated in the following way:
WER= \frac{S+D+I}{N}
where: 
* S: substitutions: reference words replaced by different ones 
* D: deletions: reference words ommitted in the predicted transcription 
* I: insertion: words added in comparison with the reference 
* N: total number of words in the reference transcription 
For the full dataset, the transcripts provided by VoxPopuli were used as a reference to evaluate Whisper's predictions. However, for the first 20 recording in each language, the manually produced transcripts served as the reference, allowing a more detailed assessment. 

ERROR ANALYSIS: 
In order to manually analyze mistakes made by the Whisper model, the following types of errors were recognized: 
* Substitutions 
* Omissions: delations
* Insertions 
Two additional subtypes were used to classify purely orthographic errors: 
* Punctuation: indicates discrepancies in punctuation marks
* Capitualization: identifies incorrect use of uppercase and lowercase letters

**RESULTS**

* English: 22.01% of total WER and 12% of sample WER,
* French: 34.41% pf total WER and 20% of sample WER,
* Italian: 38.19% of total WER and 21% of sample WER,
* Polish: 32.26% of total WER and 26% of sample WER. 
The quantitative results are presented in a clearer format in final_validation.html

The differences between the overall WER and the sample WER are partly influenced by the quality of the reference transcriptions provided by VoxPopuli. In some casesm the otherwise correct transcripts were not accurately aligned with the audio recordings, causing parts of otherwise correct transcriptions to be counted as errors. This is one of the main limitations of the analysis, which was partially addressed by manually transcribing a sample of 20 recordings per language.

Errors common to all four languages include frequent punctuation mistakes and difficulties in correctly identifying and transcribing named entities, such as people, organizations and places. These difficulties may arise because Whisper does not always recognize such entities as accurately as a human listener familiar with the context in question. This highlights the importance of manually checking the provided transcriptions. Furthermore, substitution errors were the most frequent type of error across all four languages in the first 20 recordings. 

For English, capitalization errors were particularly common. Uppercase letters were sometimes used rather inconsistently, which lead to a lot of substitution errors, especially in nouns. Nevertheless, Whisper achieved the best overall performance in this language. 

In French, many errors were related to the complexity of French orthography. Words were often substituted with different ones that did not make sense in the given context but sounded phonetically similar. French pronounciation contributed to other types of errors, including frequent substitutions of plural forms with singular ones, since the final -s is rarely pronounced. Moreover, for the same reason, one could notice some conjugation mistakes, particularly confusion between the singular and plural forms of the third person; gender errors, in which  masculine forms were recognized as feminine; and grammatical ones. One recurring example of the last type was the substitution of the Conditionnel Présent with the Futur Simple, likely due to the similar pronounciation of the endings -ais and -ai in the first person singular.

In Italian, many foreign proper nouns were incorrectly recognized as if they were Italian words and adapted to Italian spelling. Moreover, the model frequently produced words that do not exist in Italian, or that, despite being valid words, did not fit the context. Another noticeable issue was the use of prepositions, including both substitution and omission. Additionally, some difficulties were observed in recognizing the correct inflectional endings. Articles were another challenging aspect of Italian, particularly in cases requiring an apostrophe. The overall WER analysis showed that Italian had the worst results among the four languages. However, this finding should be interpreted with caution, as the issue of reference transcripts that did not allign with the audio recordings was particulatly pronounced in Italian. This was also reflected in the substantional improvement observed when manual transcriptions were used as references. 

Finally, in Polish, the grammatical aspect that caused the most difficulties was declension. The model sometimes struggled to identify the appropriate case for a noun or adjective based on the surrounding context and verb. Similarly to Italian, many words were transcribed as meaningless combinations of phonemes that sounded similar to the original words but had no meaning in Polish. Furthermore, many expressions that should have been written as separate words were merged into a single word. Noticeable substitution errors also occurred in words with more comples spelling. Wlthough Whisper performed relatively well in Polish in the overall analysis, partly due to the good alignment between the VoxPopuli reference transcripts and the recordings, it achieved the worst results in the sample analysis with manual transcriptions as references.
